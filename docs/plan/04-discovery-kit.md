# Deliverable 2: Discovery Kit

_Version 1, 2026-11-19. Use with the ranked assumptions in [03-lean-canvas.md](03-lean-canvas.md) section 6._

**Goal of discovery:** in 8 weeks, learn whether payers and clinics have this problem, how they handle it today, who owns the budget, and what a small vendor must show to get a pilot. The output is evidence for or against each assumption, plus 2–3 organisations willing to discuss a pilot.

**Not the goal:** pitching. No demo in the first 25 minutes of any call.

## 1. Targets

| Segment | UAE | India | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payers (insurer, TPA, employer benefits) | 6 | 4 | 10 |
| Clinic chains, diabetes centres, labs | 4 | 4 | 8 |
| Practising diabetologists / endocrinologists | 2 | 3 | 5 |
| Patients and caregivers | 5 | 10 | 15 |

Doctors count double: they are interviewees and clinical-advisor candidates. Expect a 10–15% reply rate on cold outreach **[Assumption]**, so the payer and clinic list needs about 120 names.

## 2. Target account criteria

Score each organisation 0–2 per row; contact those scoring 7+ first.

### Payers

| Criterion | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chronic population | Large diabetic membership, enhanced plans | Mixed book | Mostly basic low-premium plans |
| Member tenure | Members stay 3+ years (government programmes, self-insured employers) | 1–3 years | Annual churn |
| Existing programme | Runs a manual disease-management programme (has budget and an owner) | Talks about wellness publicly | Nothing visible |
| Size and speed | Mid-size, or has an innovation team | Large with a formal vendor process | Very large, global procurement |
| Data readiness | Holds its own claims data in-house | Through a TPA | Unknown |

### Clinics, diabetes centres and labs

| Criterion | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chronic focus | Diabetes or endocrinology is a core service | General practice with many chronic patients | Mostly acute or cosmetic |
| Size | 3–30 sites, owner reachable | Single large site | Hospital group with its own app |
| Follow-up pain | Recall done manually | Basic EMR reminders | Dedicated recall team and software |
| Tech openness | Uses a modern EMR, has tried digital tools | Paper plus basic software | Hostile to change |
| Payer ties | In several insurer networks | Few | Cash only |

### Who to ask for

| Organisation | Best first contact | Also useful |
|---|---|---|
| Insurer / TPA | Medical director; head of care or disease management | Head of digital or innovation; chief actuary; head of claims |
| Employer | Head of benefits or HR; company doctor | CFO for self-insured groups |
| Clinic chain | Founder or operations head; lead diabetologist | Front-desk manager (knows the real recall process) |
| Lab / pharmacy chain | Head of partnerships or digital | Operations head |

## 3. Getting meetings with no contacts

Do these in order; each one feeds the next.

- [ ] **Faculty and university network.** Ask professors, the incubator office and alumni for two introductions each. Warm introductions convert far better than cold messages.
- [ ] **Doctors first.** Practising diabetologists are easier to reach than insurer executives, and they know the medical directors. End every doctor interview by asking for an introduction.
- [ ] **LinkedIn outreach** with the message below; 10 new messages per founder per day.
- [ ] **Events.** Arab Health / WHX in Dubai, insurance and health-tech meetups, incubator demo days. **[Verify]** dates. Aim for 5 conversations per event, booked in advance where possible.
- [ ] **Incubator and regulator innovation programmes.** Apply early; some arrange introductions to payers. **[Verify]** which accept student teams.
- [ ] **Patients:** family, community groups, and patients of the doctors interviewed (with the doctor's agreement).

### Outreach message (LinkedIn or email, under 90 words)

> Hi Dr [Name], I'm a student founder at [university] researching how [insurers / clinics] in [UAE / India] keep diabetic [members / patients] on schedule with tests and medication. I'm not selling anything. I'd value 20 minutes of your experience running [their programme or role] to understand what works and what doesn't. Would a short call next week be possible? Happy to share a summary of what we learn across all interviews.

Follow up once after 5 working days, then stop.

## 4. Interview rules

1. Ask about **what they did**, not what they would do. "Tell me about the last time…" beats "Would you use…".
2. Ask for numbers and documents: the report they send to management, the spreadsheet they use.
3. Never describe the product before the last 5 minutes.
4. Two people on each call where possible: one asks, one writes.
5. Ask permission to record. If refused, take notes and write them up within an hour.
6. Do not collect any patient-identifiable data. If someone offers real patient records, decline.
7. Always end with: "Who else should I talk to?"

## 5. Interview scripts (30 minutes each)

### 5a. Payer (medical director, care management lead)

**Opening (2 min):** who we are, that it's research not sales, ask to record.

**Their world (8 min)**
1. What does your team's work on chronic members look like in a typical month?
2. Roughly how many diabetic members do you have, and how do you identify them?
3. Which members worry you most, and how do you find out they are in trouble?

**Current solution (8 min)**
4. Walk me through the last outreach you ran for diabetic members. Who did it, through which channel, what happened?
5. What tools or vendors do you use for this today? What do they cost, roughly, and how are they paid?
6. What have you tried that didn't work? Why did it stop?
7. Do members use your app? What for?

**Measurement and money (7 min)**
8. Which numbers do you report upward on chronic care, and how often?
9. If a programme claimed it improved adherence, what evidence would your actuary accept?
10. How long does a typical member stay with you? Does that affect what you invest in prevention?
11. Whose budget would a programme like this come from? When is that budget set?

**Buying and data (4 min)**
12. Tell me about the last time you brought in a small or new vendor. How long did it take and what did security and legal ask for?
13. Could a vendor receive eligibility and claims data for a pilot? What would have to be true?

**Close (1 min):** who else should we talk to? May we come back with a summary and a follow-up question?

### 5b. Clinic chain or diabetes centre (owner, operations head, lead doctor)

**Their world**
1. How many diabetic patients do you see regularly, and how often should they come back?
2. What share actually returns on schedule? How do you know?
3. Tell me about a recent patient who disappeared and came back worse.

**Current solution**
4. How do you remind patients today? Who does it, and how much time does it take?
5. What does your EMR do for recall? What's missing?
6. How often do patients bring results from other hospitals? What do you do with them? How often do you repeat a test because you couldn't see the old one?

**Money and buying**
7. What is a returning chronic patient worth to the clinic per year, roughly?
8. What software do you pay for now, and how much? Who decides?
9. How do insurers affect which patients come to you? Would being a preferred clinic in an insurer programme matter?

**Close:** who else should we talk to? Would you be open to trying something with a small group of patients?

### 5c. Doctor (diabetologist / endocrinologist)

1. What do you wish you knew about a patient before they walk in?
2. How do patients show you past results? How long does that take in a consultation?
3. Which follow-up tests and screenings get missed most? Why, in your view?
4. Which guideline do you follow for test intervals? Would your clinic's protocol differ?
5. What would make you distrust a patient app's data? What would a lab value need to show for you to rely on it?
6. Where would an app cross a line clinically for you?
7. Would you consider advising a team building this? What would you want from that role?

### 5d. Patient or caregiver

1. Tell me about your last doctor visit for diabetes. What did you take with you?
2. Where are your reports right now? Show me how you'd find your last HbA1c result. _(Watch, don't help.)_
3. Do you know if your numbers are better or worse than a year ago? How?
4. Tell me about the last time you missed a test, a refill or a dose. What happened?
5. What reminders do you use today? Who helps you keep track?
6. Which apps do you use for health? When did you last open one?
7. If your insurer offered a health app, what would you want to know before using it? Would you upload reports to it? Why or why not?
8. Which language would you want it in?

## 7. Synthesis

Review every Friday. Keep a single tracker (spreadsheet or `docs/discovery/tracker.md`) with one row per organisation: score, contact, status, interview date, pilot interest.

| Assumption (from lean canvas) | Supported if | Rejected if |
|---|---|---|
| Payers have budget and an owner | 4 of 10 name a budget line and a person | Fewer than 2 can |
| Payers will share claims data with a small vendor | 3 of 10 describe a workable route | All require certifications we can't get within a year |
| Savings within one policy year justify a fee | 3 of 10 confirm short-term cost matters and can size it | Tenure objection is raised and nobody counters it |
| Clinics will pay for recall and a summary | 3 of 8 pay for comparable software or ask for pricing | Recall is seen as "free" staff work |
| Patients will install and upload | 8 of 15 can't find their last result and say they'd try it | Most already have a system that works |
| Privacy firewall matters | Half of patients raise insurer access unprompted or hesitate at question 7 | Nobody cares |

**Decision point at week 8:** if the payer assumptions fail but the clinic ones hold, the first buyer changes to clinics and the brief is amended. If both fail, stop and rethink before building beyond the upload pipeline.

## 8. Eight-week schedule

| Week | Work |
|---|---|
| 1 | Build the 120-name target list and score it. Ask faculty and the incubator for introductions. Send first 50 messages. |
| 2 | First doctor and patient interviews. Refine scripts after the first three. |
| 3–6 | 4–6 interviews per week. Friday synthesis. Keep sending 10 messages a day. |
| 7 | Follow-up calls with the highest pilot-interest contacts; show the clickable demo for the first time. |
| 8 | Write the discovery summary; decide on first buyer and first pilot candidate; send the promised summary to interviewees. |
